## 📌 Module Overview

**Lecture 20** establishes the rigorous mathematical foundation of **Probability Theory for Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning**.
Real-world data is inherently stochastic, subject to sensor noise, incomplete observability, and natural entropy.
From defining sample spaces and Kolmogorov's axioms to deriving Bayes' Theorem and mastering parametric distributions (Binomial, Uniform, Gaussian), this module equips engineers with the probabilistic calculus required to design, train, and calibrate modern AI systems.

### Core Pillars Covered:
- **Foundations & Set Theory:** Random experiments, sample spaces, events, and Kolmogorov's three axioms.
- **Probability Calculus:** Complement rule, addition rule, multiplication rule, and the probability chain rule.
- **Conditional Inference:** Conditional probability, Law of Total Probability, and Bayesian belief revision.
- **Random Variables & Moments:** Discrete/Continuous variables, PMF, PDF, CDF, Expectation $\mathbb{E}[X]$, and Variance $\text{Var}(X)$.
- **Parametric Distributions:** Binomial, Continuous Uniform, and the Gaussian (Normal) Distribution with Z-scores.

## 📐 Mathematical Foundations Reference

### 1. Kolmogorov's Axioms of Probability
For event space $\mathcal{F}$ over sample space $\mathcal{S}$:

$$
\boxed{P(A) \ge 0 \quad \forall A \subseteq \mathcal{S}}
$$

$$
\boxed{P(\mathcal{S}) = 1.0}
$$

$$
\boxed{A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset \quad \forall i \ne j \implies P\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^\infty A_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^\infty P(A_i)}
$$

---

### 2. General Addition and Multiplication Rules

$$
\boxed{P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)}
$$

$$
\boxed{P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B \mid A) = P(B) \cdot P(A \mid B)}
$$

---

### 3. Bayes' Theorem & Law of Total Probability
For partition $\{B_1, \dots, B_k\}$ of $\mathcal{S}$ and arbitrary observed evidence $E$:

$$
\boxed{P(E) = \sum_{j=1}^k P(E \mid B_j) \cdot P(B_j)}
$$

$$
\boxed{P(B_i \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid B_i) \cdot P(B_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^k P(E \mid B_j) \cdot P(B_j)}}
$$

---

### 4. Continuous Gaussian (Normal) Distribution
For random variable $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$:

$$
\boxed{f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left( -\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \right) \qquad\text{and}\qquad Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}}
$$
